Bird flu

Birds killed in confirmed commercial and backyard flocks during the H5N1 outbreak, from USDA’s detection records.

This one is worth reading twice. It spent months showing 174.8 million birds and a note saying its source had stalled — true, but the wrong conclusion. Birds killed in the bird-flu outbreak

Birds killed in confirmed commercial and backyard flocks during the H5N1 outbreak. The weekly count sits on its own axis on the right: a single commercial layer flock can be several million birds, so the weeks are spiky, and on the running total’s scale they would be a flat line along the bottom. The download splits each week into commercial and backyard birds.

This chart read “source stalled” until recently, and the reason is worth keeping. It was fed by a CDC mirror that still returns a perfectly valid file and stopped being updated in June 2025 — nothing failed, the numbers simply stopped moving. USDA had put the data behind a Tableau dashboard with no obvious download. It turned out the dashboard publishes a second hidden view, and asking for that view as CSV returns the whole detection table. Thirty-three million birds were missing from this chart.
