COVID, influenza and RSV

Respiratory illness

Weekly hospital admissions for COVID-19, influenza and RSV, nationally and state by state.

National figures come from the CDC’s National Healthcare Safety Network, which hospitals report to each week; the series runs from August 2020. State-level influenza comes from the CDC’s FluSight forecasting hub.

All of these count laboratory-confirmed admissions rather than everyone admitted with the illness, so they undercount by a margin that moves with how much testing is being done. They read more reliably as a trend than as a total.

US respiratory hospital admissions, weekly

New hospital admissions each week with a laboratory-confirmed diagnosis, summed nationally. Influenza and RSV are reported in the same returns and are shown alongside COVID-19.

A different series for the same measure, from the archived reichlab forecast hub, was widely used up to April 2024 and runs about 4% lower at the median — the two programmes count admissions under different criteria. Figures read off older charts will not match these. That series is included in the download for comparison.

Influenza admissions, state by state

The same admissions state by state, laid out roughly as the map is, so a bad week across one region reads as a block of color. It opens on percentage change against four weeks earlier, which is what makes a small state climbing fast as visible as a large one; the buttons switch to admissions per 100,000 over a window.

Every panel shares one vertical scale. Per head rather than raw counts is what makes that readable — on raw admissions the spread between the largest and smallest states is more than fiftyfold, and most of the grid flattens into slivers.

COVID and flu patients in hospital

Patients in hospital on a given day, summed across reporting states, from the HHS hospital-capacity dataset. This is a census rather than a count of admissions — everyone in a bed that day, however long they have been there — so it runs far higher than the admissions series above and should not be read against it. HHS ended the reporting requirement behind it, so the series stops in April 2024.
