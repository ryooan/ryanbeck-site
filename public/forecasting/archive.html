rescued data

The archive

Several of these series outlived the places they came from. Where a source has gone for good the history is kept here rather than lost with it.

What is in it

124 files, about 18 MB, one CSV per data frame, gzipped. The manifest lists every one with its row count, column count and date range.

The largest single item is the full HHS hospital-capacity panel — 66,966 rows by 135 columns, every state, 2020 to 2023, including age-banded admissions. That is the best age-stratified COVID series that survives anywhere for Virginia, and it is the reason the retired VDH cases-by-age page points at it.

Why it exists

Because the old dashboards fetched, analysed, plotted and published in one step, nothing was ever kept. A rendered PNG was committed; the numbers behind it were not. When a source went away, so did its history.

No source data was ever committed to the old repository — checked across all 9,026 of its commits. What survived did so by accident, in R session files on one machine, and for several of these series that was the only copy in existence. Extracting them was the first thing done here and the only step that got more expensive by waiting.
