Forecasting data

Numbers I kept reaching for while forecasting, collected so I would stop re-downloading them by hand. Each series below refreshes itself on a schedule, and each one states the date its data actually ends — which is not always the date it last ran.

That distinction is the point. A scheduled job reporting success tells you it fetched something, not that anyone upstream is still publishing. One of these pages ran green every ten hours for two years while showing a series that had stopped; the status line on each chart now comes from the data itself, so it cannot make that claim.

US debt limit

Federal debt subject to the statutory ceiling, from Treasury's Daily Treasury Statement, against the ceiling itself. The shaded stretches are the eight periods when Congress suspended the limit rather than raising it — during a suspension there is no number to plot, so the ceiling line stops.

5,246 rows, latest up to date · source: Treasury Fiscal Data, Daily Treasury Statement · download: debt-limit.csv

US refugee admissions

People admitted to the United States as refugees each month, from the State Department's Refugee Processing Center. Fiscal years run October to September; the annual ceiling is set by presidential determination and is a cap, not a target.

310 rows, latest 53 days ago · source: State Department Refugee Processing Center · download: refugee-admissions.csv · refugee-admissions-annual.csv

Big-tech combined revenue

Annual revenue of Alphabet, Amazon, Apple, Meta and Microsoft, read straight out of their SEC filings. Billions of dollars. The five do not share a fiscal calendar — Apple's year ends in September and Microsoft's in June — so the combined line is blank for any year where they have not all filed yet.

20 rows, latest about 1.6 years ago · source: SEC XBRL company facts · download: faamg-revenues.csv

US semiconductor exports to China

Monthly US exports to China of semiconductors and of the equipment used to make them, from UN Comtrade. This one was dead for three years: the old Comtrade endpoint was retired and the code that built the page had been deleted from the repo. The code was recovered from git history and the series now runs on the current API.

150 rows, latest about 3 months ago · source: UN Comtrade · download: semiconductors-us-exports-china.csv

Birds killed in confirmed commercial and backyard flocks during the H5N1 outbreak. Weekly counts are spiky because a single commercial layer flock can be several million birds on its own, so the running total is the clearer line.

The archive

Several of these series outlived the places they came from. Where a source has gone for good the history is kept here rather than lost with it — 124 files of it, pulled out of old R session files that were, for a few of these, the only surviving copy.

Browse the archive manifest — every file with its row count and date range.
