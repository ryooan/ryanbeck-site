Two ways to build the book

Same pages, same leather, same paper. The only difference is what is doing the work: hand-written CSS and canvas on the left, StPageFlip and tsParticles on the right. Turn a few pages in each — and on the right, try dragging a corner.

Hand-built

CSS 3D + canvas

Eight nested slices for the curl, a hand-written particle system for the dust. No dependencies.

Chapter the first

The short version

I write science fiction, I think about forecasting for a living, and I have never once managed to have only one hobby at a time.

Placeholder copy — rewrite this in your own voice. The through-line, if there is one, is a stubborn interest in the gap between a model and the thing the model is about. Bridges taught me that gap has consequences. Forecasting taught me how to put a number on it. Fiction is where I get to ask what happens when nobody bothers.

Everything in this book is scaffolding until you replace it.

Chapter the second

Before this

For about a decade I designed and inspected bridges across Iowa.

It is a profession that makes you honest. A load path either exists or it doesn't. A tolerance is not a matter of opinion. You learn to ask what you would have to believe for your own analysis to be wrong, and then you go and check, because the alternative is unacceptable.

I think about that habit constantly in work that has far softer feedback.

Page 2

Chapter the third

Now

These days I coordinate forecasting programs at Metaculus.

Most of the job is not prediction. It is writing questions that can actually resolve, arguing about wording until the ambiguity is gone, and then finding out in public whether the crowd was right. It turns out that "what exactly would count as this happening?" is one of the more useful questions you can ask about almost anything.

Calibration is a habit, not a talent.

Page 3

Chapter the fourth

The fiction

SEER began as a question I couldn't put down.

If a system could reliably predict what you wanted — not guess, not nudge, but know — would you still call it wanting? The novel follows Maria, who finds the seam in it, and then has to decide whether tearing it open is worth what comes after.

Not Human is a shorter, colder cousin of the same question: the first person to have her mind copied to a machine, and a copy that insists it is her.

Chapter the fifth

The Missing Nuance

The newsletter is where the arguments go.

It's named for the thing that gets sanded off when a position is repeated enough times — the caveat that made it true in the first place. Mostly forecasting, occasionally technology, occasionally an essay I couldn't place anywhere else.

themissingnuance.substack.com

Off the clock

Married, with daughters, in Iowa.

The serial-hobbyist thing is not a bit. There is always some half-learned skill in progress and a shelf of evidence from the last one. Reading, mostly; building things that didn't need to exist; and a persistent belief that this is the year I finish everything I start.

What actually differs

 Hand-builtStPageFlip + tsParticles
Ships~4 KB of JS, no dependencies233 KB minified / 65 KB gzipped, 5 packages
The foldEight rotated slices approximating a cylinderProper fold geometry with a computed crease
Drag a cornerNo — click or arrow keys onlyYes, and it follows the pointer
ShadowsHand-placed gradientsDrawn from the fold, so they track it
DustEmits from the moving free edge, inherits its velocityEmitters fire on flip events; not tied to the page position
TuningAnything, but you write itA config object, but only what it exposes
In three yearsStill works; it's plain CSSNeeds the packages to still install and behave
